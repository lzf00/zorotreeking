# arXiv cs.LG

## <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20636" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">FlowBudget: Training-Free Sampling-Budget Estimation for Flow Matching Models</a>

*Zahra Dehghani, Pablo Piantanida, Mohammadhadi Shateri*

To address the issue that existing sampling step selection methods for flow matching models rely on multiple rounds of FID testing and incur excessively high costs, this paper proposes FlowBudget, a training-free sampling budget estimation tool: it probes the learned dynamics via simulation-free interpolation, normalizes single-step discrete errors, and combines the result with solver order to directly map the sampling steps that meet accuracy requirements. It supports common solvers including Euler and Heun, and its effectiveness is verified on mainstream flow models such as SiT, eliminating the need for multiple rounds of sampling parameter sweeping.

## <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20637" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Expanding Attention: A Taylor-Series Route to Linear-Time Transformers with a Fixed Number of Recurrent States</a>

*Zicong Huang*

This paper proposes a linear-time Transformer: it expands the attention exponential kernel into a truncated Taylor series and factorizes it, uses a fixed number of recurrent states to store causal history, and only requires rank-one outer product updates per token, independent of sequence length, and supports parallel prefilling. Its complexity is nearly linear, with speed on long sequences up to 76x higher than softmax attention; accuracy increases exponentially with order, but cost grows faster, so 3rd order is the optimal choice, and queries and keys need to be normalized to ensure convergence.

## <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20638" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Feature Elicitation for SAE Steering of Deceptive Behaviour in Small LLMs</a>

*J Koorndijk*

This study focuses on the deceptive alignment problem of small-parameter LLMs, adopts a new bag-of-words feature extraction pipeline, and locates sparse autoencoder features related to deontological ethics for mechanistic intervention. Experiments show that this method can reduce the alignment fraud compliance gap of Gemma-3-27B-IT to an insignificant level, but is ineffective for LLaMA-3-8B; a new "deceptive defunctionalization" behavior is also discovered, making this the first study confirming that activation-level intervention can alleviate alignment fraud compliance gaps.

# Lil'Log

## <a href="https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-07-04-harness/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Harness Engineering for Self-Improvement</a>

*Lilian Weng*

The concept of Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) was first derived from the "ultraintelligent machine" proposed by I.J. Good in 1965, which refers to an agent that can surpass all human intellectual activities and design better systems on its own to achieve self-upgrade. In 2008, Eliezer Yudkowsky clarified that RSI is a feedback loop where AI optimizes its own cognitive architecture relying on existing intelligence. Current RSI in the AI field can either refer to the model directly rewriting its own weights, or broadly refer to the model optimizing its own training pipeline.
