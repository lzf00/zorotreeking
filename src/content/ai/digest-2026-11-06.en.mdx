# Hugging Face Daily Papers

## <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00883" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Random Is Almost Enough? Stable and Low-Cost Benchmark Compression via BestRand</a>

*HF ★ 13 · George Ling, Shanshan Zhong, Lijin Yang… · [HF Mirror](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.00883)*

To reduce the cost of model benchmark evaluation, this study first derives the distribution of estimation errors for benchmark subsets, confirming that random sampling performs well but has inherent issues of large long-tail errors and poor stability. Based on this, the BestRand compression method is proposed, which retains the high efficiency of random sampling while cutting off the high-error tail. Verified by over 70 types of benchmarks, this method reduces high-error risk by 50.2% compared to existing solutions, cuts average estimation error by 16.4%, has only 51.3% of the construction cost of previous solutions, and reduces average error in out-of-distribution scenarios by 21.8%, delivering better overall performance.

## <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07854" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Towards Foundation Decision Models: Drex</a>

*HF ★ 10 · Nischay Dhankhar, Alisher Amantay, Zhanibek Datbayev… · [HF Mirror](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.07854)*

Aiming at the pain points of unstable autoregressive inference output of large models, required output parsing, and high latency cost, this paper launches 4 open-source decision models of the Drex series, which can directly output option probability distributions without autoregressive decoding. The models are trained with supervised fine-tuning + RLAF, and the top-tier Max version is additionally optimized for vision, with performance comparable to commercial large models such as GPT-6 Sol, several times faster inference speed, and stable output, making it the best open-source decision model of its kind currently available.

## <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">EDGAR: Efficient Diffusion-Driven Gaussian Avatar Refinement via Synthetic Failure</a>

*HF ★ 7 · Kwan Yun, Minhyeok Bang, Colette Suhjung Koo… · [HF Mirror](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.07909)*

Aiming at defects like blurring, stretching, and temporal instability in Gaussian head avatars reconstructed from monocular views, this paper proposes the EDGAR diffusion optimization framework: a ternary training set containing simulated failure samples is constructed, identity features extracted by the Sapiens self-supervised model are injected into the single-step diffusion + MoE structure to adapt to different degradations, and the optimized unified Gaussian model does not require frame-by-frame calling. Multiple experiments verify that its perceptual quality, reconstruction accuracy, cross-view consistency, and robustness to extreme poses are all better than existing baselines. (Full text 121 words)

## <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04894" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">VisSearch-Bench: Evaluating Long Horizon Visual Search in Multimodal Agents</a>

*HF ★ 3 · ZhengXian Wu, Qianhao Nie, Zhuohong Chen… · [HF Mirror](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.04894)*

Existing visual search evaluations for multimodal agents mostly fail to measure the ability to associate visual information across images and steps. To address this gap, this study launches the VisSearch-Bench evaluation set, covering 204 manually written long-horizon visual search tasks across 7 major fields, with complete supporting evidence, answers, and two-dimensional evaluation rules. Tests on 16 models show that even with search tools equipped, the best system only achieves a 26.47% task completion rate, highlighting the significant technical challenges of this type of task.

## <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06817" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Open-HumanScale: An Open Recipe for Scaling Human and Robot Experience for Embodied Foundation Models</a>

*HF ★ 2 · Juncheng Ma, Jiaxin Li, Jiankai Tu… · [HF Mirror](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06817)*

This paper launches Open-HumanScale, an open-source solution for embodied foundation models, paired with a 50,000-hour annotated library of first-person human operation videos, empirically deriving a log-linear scaling law between pre-training data volume and downstream robot task success rate. Two types of world action models trained based on this scheme: the version pre-trained with pure human data delivers excellent performance, while the human-robot hybrid pre-trained version refreshes SOTA on multiple benchmarks, with zero-shot generalization and embodied in-context learning capabilities. All resources have been open-sourced.

## <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06953" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">SteelAgent: LLM-Orchestrated, Physics-Informed Steel Property Prediction with Audited Generalization and Calibrated Uncertainty</a>

*Aleksandr Volkov, Roman Sultimov, Mikhail Kuzin…*

This paper proposes SteelAgent: an LLM-orchestrated steel property prediction framework integrated with metallurgical physical information, supporting performance prediction, uncertainty output, and reverse design. Tests show that the model's generalization ability is greatly affected by dataset splitting methods: under random splitting, the gradient boosting model achieves an R² of 0.859, which plummets to 0.113 during steel family hold-out validation, with uncertainty calibration greatly degraded, and physical feature gains fluctuating across scenarios. The study recommends that performance reports clearly mark the evaluation sample population.

## <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06956" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Octopus: One Brain, Many Branches for Latent-KV Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning</a>

*Tianming Sha*

Aiming at the problems of redundant KV cache and lost cross-branch dependencies in existing multi-agent large model frameworks, this paper proposes the multi-agent reinforcement learning framework Octopus, relying on a shared backbone and directed acyclic graph of KV states, adopting three mechanisms: zero-copy branching, cross-branch KV mounting, and DAG structure credit allocation. On retrieval tasks, accuracy is improved by up to 17.5 percentage points compared to baselines, training throughput is increased by 3.3x, and peak KV memory usage is reduced by 2.8x.

## <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06979" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Uncertainty calibration in Bayesian M/EEG source imaging</a>

*Ismail Huseynov, Mohammad Orabe, Srikantan Nagarajan…*

Aiming at the lack of calibration of posterior credible intervals in M/EEG source imaging, this paper proposes a simulation-driven Bayesian inversion uncertainty quantification calibration framework, comparing Bayesian minimum norm with sLORETA weighting and sparse Bayesian learning method with S-FLEX extension, paired with isotonic recalibration post-processing. Simulations and real-world measurements show that before recalibration, the sparse scheme has more accurate positioning and uncertainty more aligned with nominal values; after recalibration, both methods are improved, and the sparse scheme delivers more focused positioning and higher statistical power in real MEG tasks.

## <a href="https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-07-04-harness/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Harness Engineering for Self-Improvement</a>

*Lilian Weng*

This paper sorts out the conceptual evolution of Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI): In 1965, I.J. Good first proposed the idea of superintelligent machines, that is, systems that can fully surpass human intelligence and independently iteratively design better machines. In 2008, Eliezer Yudkowsky clearly defined RSI: the feedback loop where AI relies on its existing intelligence to optimize its own cognitive mechanisms. In the current AI context, such feedback loops can be manifested as models directly rewriting their own weights, or broadly refer to autonomous optimization of training processes.
