This is not a trip report — it is a demonstration of what a route page looks like here. The track is synthetic
(25 points, elevation 400–1120 m), so it shows the rendering without claiming to be real hiking data.

A real route page has four parts. The stats bar on top carries three numbers computed straight from the GPX file:
total distance, cumulative ascent and estimated walking time. Below it, the track is drawn on an OpenStreetMap
basemap and you can pan and zoom it; waypoints mark junctions and resupply points. An elevation profile follows,
distance on the horizontal axis and altitude on the vertical one, so the steep sections stand out and you can see
where the day will actually hurt. Last comes the day-by-day itinerary and the pre-trip judgement: permits, season,
somewhere to sleep, and a plan for turning back — none of which a map can tell you.

To add a route, provide two things: a GPX file in `public/gpx/` and a paragraph or two about weather, trail
conditions, gear and what you would do differently. Distance, ascent and altitude come from the build step rather
than from hand-typed numbers, so the page can never disagree with its own track.
